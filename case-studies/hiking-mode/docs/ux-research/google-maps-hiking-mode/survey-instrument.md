# Survey instrument: Google Maps Hiking Mode

Status: Synthesized case study instrument (not fielded with real respondents)

## Purpose
Size tool-switching and Maps abandonment on rugged terrain, rank on-trail failure stress, measure day-commit must-haves, and triangulate with coded public commentary from Google Maps trail reviews and outdoor social channels — to inform whether Hiking mode should be backpacker-led (continuity + safety) or day-hike discovery-led.

## Screening
1. In the last 24 months, have you used a smartphone map while hiking or mountain sport? (Yes / No — end if No)
2. Longest trip in that period: Day hike / Weekend (1 night) / Multi-day (2–6 nights) / Long-distance section or thru-hike (7+ nights)
3. Have you used Google Maps on any outdoor trip in that period? (Yes / No — continue either way; segment later)

## Core questions

### S1 — Tool mix (RQ1)
On your most recent overnight or longer trip, which did you use? (multi-select)
- Google Maps
- Apple Maps
- AllTrails
- Gaia GPS / similar
- FarOut / Guthook-style
- Offline GPX on another app
- Paper map / guidebook only

Metric: % using Google Maps + at least one specialty trail app on the same trip.

### S2 — Stage use of Google Maps (RQ1)
When did you open Google Maps on that trip? (multi-select)
- Planning at home
- Driving to trailhead
- On trail navigation
- Finding water / camp
- Town / resupply
- Not used

Metric: Stage-use rates; drop-off from road/trailhead stages to on-trail dirt stages.

### S3 — Abandonment reason (RQ1)
If you stopped using Google Maps once terrain got rugged, what was the main reason? (single)
- Wrong trail line / missing footpaths
- No offline
- Weak elevation / grade
- No water / camp / bailouts
- Habit / other app preferred
- Did not stop

Metric: Primary abandonment reason distribution among backpacker-primary respondents.

### S4 — Stress events (RQ2)
Which caused the most stress on trail? Pick up to 3.
- Wrong junction / lost trail
- Harder elevation than expected
- Dry or unreliable water
- No clear bailout to a road
- Map unavailable offline
- Conflicting info across apps
- Weather change without a plan

Metric: Top-3 selection rate per failure mode among backpacker-primary respondents.

### S5 — Day-commit must-haves (RQ3)
To commit to tomorrow’s hiking day, which are must-have? (select all)
- Distance
- Elevation gain / loss
- Trail difficulty or grade
- Water sources
- Campsites
- Bailout / exit to road
- Weather / advisory
- Resupply / town timing

Metric: % “must-have” per item.

### S6 — Trust (RQ4)
Rate trust 1–5: navigating paved roads in Google Maps; navigating dirt trails in Google Maps; navigating dirt trails in your primary specialty app.

Metric: Mean trust scores; dirt-vs-road gap for Maps.

### S7 — Segment & lighter mode (RQ5)
Trip-length segment (from screen) plus: would a simpler Hiking mode in Maps help versus road-only view? (Yes / No / Not sure)

Metric: Day/weekend vs backpacker agreement on simpler mode; overload flags.

### S8 — Hiking mode intent (RQ6)
If Google Maps added a Hiking mode for multi-day trail planning, offline corridor download, elevation, and bailouts, how likely would you use it as your primary on-trail map? (1–5)

Metric: % rating 4–5 among backpacker-primary.

## Social listening protocol

### Sources
- Google Maps place and trailhead reviews mentioning hiking, trail, offline, or “got lost”
- Reddit communities (r/hiking, r/CampingandHiking, r/Ultralight, regional trail subs)
- Trail app forums and GPX sharing threads
- Public posts/comments on X and Instagram outdoor accounts referencing Maps vs trail apps

### Coding focus
- Abandonment phrases: “switched to…”, “Maps took me off trail”, “useless after trailhead”
- Failure modes: wrong turn, no offline, dry water, elevation surprise, no bailout
- Trust language: roads vs dirt; “would never rely on Maps in the mountains”
- Day-commit cues: miles, gain, water, camp, weather before sleep
- Consolidation desire: “wish Maps did hiking”, “too many apps”

### Synthesized yield (Hypothesis)
Coded public language clustered around trailhead handoff, wrong-turn blame on road routing, offline/download gaps on remote ridges, missing elevation honesty, and bailout/exit questions in storm or injury threads. Continuity and safety dominate over discovery praise.

## Sample framing (synthesized)
Target n≈120 completers (delivered as n≈124 in findings); analyze backpacker-primary vs day/weekend separately for RQ5.
