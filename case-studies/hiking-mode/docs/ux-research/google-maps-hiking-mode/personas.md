# Personas: Google Maps Hiking Mode

Status: Provisional hypothesis (synthesized research)

## Primary: Maya Chen

- Role and context: Thru-section backpacker (28–40), planning and hiking multi-day corridors with a full pack; uses Google Maps for roads, lodging, and resupply towns between dirt stretches.
- Goal: Finish each section day on plan and get to town safely without navigation chaos.
- Job to be done: When I’m moving for days on a long trail, I want continuous trail-aware planning and navigation in one place, so I can manage miles, water, camp, and exits without juggling apps.
- Behaviors: Mixes FarOut/AllTrails/Gaia + GPX + Maps; screenshots key points; plans days by miles and elevation; downloads offline maps before remote stretches.
- Pains: Trail continuity breaks after trailhead; elevation surprises; scattered water/camp/bailout data; offline fear; app switching on tired evenings.
- Needs: Multi-day trail continuity; day-commit info (distance, gain, water, camp, bailout); offline corridor confidence; clear path back to roads/towns; Maps-native patterns she already knows.
- Quote: Hypothesis, hypothetical — “If Maps could carry me from the interstate to my tent site with the same confidence, I’d delete two apps tomorrow.”
- Design implication: Hiking mode must prioritize long-route continuity, day planning, safety exits, and offline readiness over discovery galleries.

## Secondary: Jordan Blake

- Role and context: Day and weekend hiker (25–45) who drives to popular trailheads, wants a clear out-and-back or loop, and occasionally camps one night.
- Goal: Pick a doable hike, stay on the path, and get back before dark.
- Job to be done: When I have a free day or weekend, I want a simple trail plan with difficulty and time, so I can enjoy the hike without getting lost or overcommitted.
- Behaviors: Searches trails in Maps or AllTrails; follows blue-dot / trail line; checks parking and estimated duration; rarely needs multi-day resupply logic.
- Pains: Hard to judge difficulty from road maps; unsure which path is the hiking trail; overwhelmed by thru-hiker jargon when tools are too advanced.
- Needs: Easy Hiking mode entry; difficulty and time estimate; clear trail emphasis; optional lighter preset that hides multi-day controls.
- Quote: Hypothesis, hypothetical — “I just need the trail to be obvious and to know if it’s too hard for me.”
- Design implication: Keep advanced backpacker tools available but default secondary users into a lighter experience so the mode stays open without overload.
